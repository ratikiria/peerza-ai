The Problem

Trading is taught by the worst possible system.

Every retail trader rebuilds the same four pieces from scratch, on platforms that weren't designed for them, with no reputation that carries between any of them.

What exists today

  • Twitter — noise, scammers, no proof of skill
  • Stocktwits — abandoned, no community pulse
  • Discord — fragmented, ephemeral, paid-gated
  • YouTube — entertainment, no feedback loop
  • Brokerages — execution only, zero learning

What traders actually need

  • Provable performance — who's actually right?
  • Real practice — without losing real money
  • Structured learning — not 60-min ramblings
  • Portable reputation — that follows you everywhere
  • A community that shows trades, not just opinions
No platform integrates these. No reputation layer survives between them. A trader can be a top-1% performer on a simulator and a nobody the moment they walk into a Discord. The category is wide open — and the winner owns the trust layer for retail investing.
Why Now

Three shifts are colliding right now.

01

Retail trading became permanent.

Retail investors traded $5.4 trillion in 2025 — up 47% year over year, the highest on record (Vanda Research). The 2020 cohort didn't leave. It compounded, and it went global.

02

The social layer degraded.

Twitter's collapse as a finance community left tens of millions of traders without a home. Discord is fragmented and paywalled. Stocktwits stalled. The audience exists; the product doesn't.

03

Provable identity got cheap.

Sub-cent settlement makes per-action reputation economically viable for the first time. What used to be a database row can now be a portable, verifiable credential the user actually owns.

The window is open because all three happened at once. A trader-native social network was possible five years ago but had no reputation primitive. A reputation primitive was possible two years ago but had no audience. Both now exist.

The Moat

The reputation graph compounds. Feeds don't.

Content is commoditised — anyone can clone a feed in a quarter. What can't be cloned is four years of verified, cross-pillar behavioural history about the same person.

01 · DATA DEPTH

Four streams, one identity.

Posts, simulated trades, lessons completed, duels won — scored together. A competitor would have to ship a social network, a trading simulator, a tutor, and a game engine before they collected the first useful data point.

02 · PORTABILITY

The user owns the score.

Settled on Solana, so the credential leaves the platform with the user. That inverts the usual walled-garden trade and makes Peerza the issuer of record rather than a silo — a position competitors can't copy without abandoning their own lock-in.

03 · DEMAND SIDE

Third parties want to read it.

Prop firms allocate capital on proven skill. Brokers price risk on it. DeFi protocols underwrite on it. Every external reader makes the score more valuable to hold — and raises switching costs for the trader.

The acid test: without the reputation layer, Peerza is a very good social app. With it, it's the trust infrastructure retail investing has never had. Already live: Ranked Calls, deadline-bound calls settled automatically against market data, scored so random guessing loses points.
